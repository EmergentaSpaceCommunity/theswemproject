## Why this agreement exists

SWEM is published under the GNU Affero General Public License, version 3 or later, to everyone.
The author alone also grants licences under other terms - commercial licences - to people who
cannot accept the AGPL's obligations.

Granting such a licence requires holding enough rights over every part of the work to grant it.
Without an agreement like this one, each contributor keeps the copyright in what they wrote, and a
licence for the whole under other terms could not be granted without asking every contributor
individually, forever - or, worse, the first merged contribution would end that offer for the
whole of SWEM. This agreement asks you for the rights that keep it possible, and gives them to the
author and to nobody else.

It does **not** ask you to give up your copyright. You keep it, and you may use and license your own
contribution, on its own, however else you wish. It gives you no rights over SWEM beyond the AGPL.

## Notes, not part of the agreement

- **Why a handle.** The Author publishes under a name, as many maintainers do; the name is
  the one on every commit of this repository. A commercial licence is a contract signed outside
  this repository, and the parties identify themselves there.
- **Individual vs. entity.** This agreement covers an individual or an entity making a
  submission; an entity submits through a person it has authorised, and section 4 is that
  person's word for it.
- **Alternative considered and set aside.** A Developer Certificate of Origin (DCO) sign-off is
  lighter on contributors and is what many AGPL projects use. It was set aside because a DCO
  certifies origin without granting the Author any right to relicense, and the offer in
  `LICENSES.md` depends on holding that right. If that offer is ever withdrawn, a DCO becomes the
  better choice and this document should be replaced by one.
- **Scope.** Every contribution to this repository, all of which is AGPL-3.0-or-later.
- **Where the burden lands.** Requiring a CLA does reduce drive-by contributions. That cost is
  accepted deliberately, in exchange for the offer staying possible.
